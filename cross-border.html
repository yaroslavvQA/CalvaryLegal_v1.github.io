Who We Serve

Built for Clients
Who Live Without Borders

Cross-border planning is a necessity for any globally mobile person or family with assets, business interests, or life circumstances that span two or more countries.

◈
US Citizens Living Abroad
Americans residing overseas face US tax obligations regardless of where they live. We coordinate foreign tax credits, treaty elections, FEIE planning, and estate structures across both countries.
◈
Foreign Nationals with US Assets
Non-resident aliens investing in US real estate or securities face US estate tax exposure. We structure holdings to minimize that exposure while maintaining investment flexibility.
◈
Dual Citizens & Green Card Holders
Subject to worldwide US taxation, these clients need optimized cross-border strategies — and, when appropriate, careful planning for expatriation or covered expatriate status.
◈
Families Relocating Internationally
Pre-immigration and pre-departure planning is essential. We restructure wealth, trusts, and business interests before a change in tax residency to avoid irreversible tax consequences.
What We Navigate

The Complexity
We're Built For

Most domestic attorneys are not equipped for the layered compliance obligations, treaty interactions, and jurisdictional conflicts of international planning. We are built precisely for this complexity.

Treaty Law
Dual Taxation & Treaty Navigation
Tax treaties reduce — but do not eliminate — double taxation. Treaty elections are highly technical. We identify the optimal treaty position for each client's fact pattern and structure accordingly.
IRS / FinCEN
FBAR, FATCA & Foreign Reporting
US persons with foreign accounts face FinCEN 114, Form 8938, Form 3520, and Form 5471 obligations. Failure to comply triggers penalties far exceeding account value. We build reporting architecture from day one.
Inheritance Law
Forced Heirship & Conflicting Laws
Civil law jurisdictions — France, Germany, Switzerland, UAE — impose mandatory inheritance shares that override private planning. We design structures that achieve client objectives within or around these constraints.
IRC § 877A
Expatriation & Exit Tax
Renouncing citizenship or abandoning a Green Card triggers the Section 877A exit tax regime. We provide pre-expatriation planning to understand exposure and minimize the exit tax where possible.
